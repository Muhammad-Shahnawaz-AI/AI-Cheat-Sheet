A friendly, practical guide for everyone — no technical background needed.

The key idea

AI is not the skill. The real skill is knowing what to ask, which tool to pick, how to describe the job clearly, and when to double-check the answer.

Part A · Foundations

Start with the right mindset

Choose an assistant for the job, not the brand name.

01 · Easy

The AI Tool Mindset

Good place to start

Before asking “Which AI is best?”, ask these seven questions:

  1. What exactly do I want done?
  2. What information does it need?
  3. Does this need reasoning, search, files, images, or automation?
  4. How important is it that the facts are correct?
  5. Does it need up-to-date information?
  6. Which tool is strongest for this specific job?
  7. How will I check the result?
Core principle: Choose the tool based on the task, not the brand name.

03 · Easy

Product vs. model vs. API

ProductThe app you chat with

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

ModelThe engine underneath

GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama

APIA way for software to connect to an engine

One engine can power many apps; one app may offer different engines. Fast models suit drafts and summaries. Reasoning models suit harder analysis but can take longer and cost more.

Choose your effort

Use the right-sized model

  • Quick draft or brainstorm: a fast, general-purpose assistant is often enough.
  • Multi-step analysis: consider a reasoning-focused model and give it clear context.
  • High-stakes facts: use authoritative sources and expert review, whatever the model.

You do not need the strongest model for every little task.

Good place to start

04Practice

Ask for everyday analogies, Socratic questions, exercises, feedback on misconceptions, and gradually harder practice.

Copy-ready tutor prompt
Teach me [TOPIC] from first principles. First assess my level with 5 questions. Then build a progressive lesson. Do not reveal answers before I attempt the exercises. After each attempt, tell me exactly what I misunderstood.
A repeatable research workflow

Go from question to grounded answer

Define your question → run deep research → open key sources → check claims → note gaps → write. AI speeds up discovery and organisation; you remain responsible for validity and conclusions.

Part C · Prompt engineering

Say what you mean. Get something useful.

A clear brief turns a vague request into an answer you can actually use.

06 · Intermediate

The 10-part prompt model

PartAsk yourself
1. RoleWhat expertise or viewpoint is useful?
2. ObjectiveWhat must be achieved?
3. ContextWhat background does the AI need?
4. InputWhat information are you providing?
5. ConstraintsWhat limits apply—length, tone, jargon?
6. ProcessWhat stages should it follow?
7. ExamplesWhat does a good answer look like?
8. OutputWhat exact format do you want?
9. EvaluationHow will you judge quality?
10. SafetyWhat needs verification or restriction?
Beginner shortcut: Start with Role + Objective + Context + Output. That already beats most one-line prompts.
A small change, a big difference

Weak vs. strong prompt

✕ Weak

“Make me a presentation about AI.”

✓ Strong

“Create a 10-slide presentation for first-year university students. Goal: explain generative AI. Each slide: one central idea, max 30 words, one suggested visual, speaker notes, and a transition to the next slide.”

07 · Intermediate

Few-shot prompting & prompt chaining

Zero-shot means just ask. Few-shot means show 2–5 examples first so the AI can follow your pattern.

Prompt chaining breaks bigger tasks into small, checkable steps:

QuestionScopeEvidenceAnalysisOutlineDraftCritiqueFinal

Smaller steps make mistakes easier to catch and correct.

08 · Advanced

Techniques for when you’re ready

Role promptingFew-shot examplesDelimitersStructured outputNegative constraintsSelf-critiqueStep-by-step reasoningMetaprompting

Clearly separate instructions from input, show examples, request a fixed format, and ask the AI to check its draft against your criteria.

09 · Intermediate

When AI makes things up

AI can invent citations, facts, quotes, numbers, or answers—and sound confident doing it.

  • Provide a source and ask it to use only that text.
  • Ask for quotes with page or section numbers.
  • Allow it to say “I don’t know.”
  • Use search for current facts; verify numbers with a calculator or code.
  • Cross-check important claims with another source.
Before you use the answer

A seven-point quality check

  • Accuracy: Are the claims correct?
  • Relevance: Does it answer the actual task?
  • Completeness: Are important requirements covered?
  • Grounding: Are key claims supported?
  • Format: Did it follow your instructions?
  • Clarity: Can the audience understand it?
  • Usability: Can you actually use the result?

15 · Intermediate

Data analysis & SQL

Good place to start

Use tools like Julius AI, Excel Copilot, or Gemini in Sheets to explore data in plain language. For more control, ask AI for Python or SQL, then run and inspect it yourself.

Safe data prompt
Here is the dataset description: [COLUMNS]. Question: [QUESTION]. First list assumptions and data-quality checks. Then provide code. Explain what each result means and its limitations. Do not state any number unless it comes from the data or executed code.
A useful mental model

What makes AI productivity work?

Productivity gain ≈ Task selection × Tool selection × Prompt quality × Workflow design × Verification.

Match the amount of checking to the stakes: low stakes → quick review; medium → source check and cross-check; high → authoritative sources and expert review.

For code: generate → test → run → review. For numbers: calculate independently and confirm.

Part F · Build

Go further with code & automation

Optional for everyone else; powerful when a task repeats or needs a custom tool.

16 · Intermediate

AI coding helpers

Good place to start

Explore GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Replit, Lovable, Bolt, or Claude Code. Use version control, write tests, review every change, and never put secrets in prompts.

Copy-ready debugging prompt
Error: [ERROR]
Code: [CODE]
Expected: [EXPECTED]
Actual: [ACTUAL]
What I already tried: [TRIED]

List the 3 most likely causes ranked by probability, how to confirm each, then give the smallest fix. Do not rewrite unrelated code.
17 · Intermediate

Automation & agents

Tools like Zapier, Make, n8n, or Power Automate connect apps: “when this happens → do that.” Add AI to classify an email or draft a response, then pause for approval.

An agent can plan steps and use tools toward a goal. More autonomy calls for tighter permissions, human approval before irreversible actions, and good logs.

18–20 · Advanced

RAG, local AI & frameworks

RAG retrieves relevant parts of your documents before generating an answer—useful for manuals, course materials, and knowledge bases.

Local AI (such as Ollama or LM Studio) runs on your own computer and can help with privacy or offline use, but needs suitable hardware.

Frameworks such as LangChain, LlamaIndex, CrewAI, and MCP are for people building custom systems. Most non-technical users never need them.

Before you automate

Keep the human in control

  • What happens if the AI is wrong?
  • Does a person need to approve the action?
  • Are permissions limited to what is necessary?
  • Can you reverse the action?
  • Are important actions logged?
Part G · Stay safe

Use AI with care and good judgement

Protect private information, check the work, and choose tools intentionally.

23 · Easy

Security & privacy golden rules

Never casually paste: passwords, API keys, confidential customer or patient data, private source code if policy forbids it, or anything you would not want stored on a third-party server.
  • Remove names and IDs; use placeholders.
  • For confidential information, prefer approved local or enterprise tools.
  • Follow academic integrity and disclosure rules.
  • Check copyright before commercial use.
  • Watch for bias and never make a deepfake without permission.
26 · Easy

The 30-second tool decision tree

  1. Needs current information? Use search or a Perplexity-style tool.
  2. Depends on your documents? Use NotebookLM or document AI.
  3. Needs code or data? Use a coding or data environment with AI.
  4. Needs image, video, or audio? Choose a specialised creative tool.
  5. Happens repeatedly? Consider automation.
If all answers are no, a general assistant is usually enough.

Build a small personal stack: one general assistant, one research tool, a coding helper if needed, a document system, a creative tool, an automation platform, and verification habits. No need to install 30 tools.

27 · Easy

Quick checks & common mistakes

Before you ask
  • Is the objective exact?
  • Is this the right tool?
  • Have I included the needed context and output format?
  • Is it safe to share?
After the answer
  • Is it correct and complete?
  • Did it follow the instructions?
  • Did it invent anything?
  • Is the result actually useful?
Common mistakes and easy fixes
  • Vague prompt: state goal, audience, format, and constraints.
  • No context: provide background, examples, and source text.
  • One giant request: break it into smaller steps.
  • Trusting the first answer: critique, then verify.
  • Pasting secrets: redact and use placeholders.
  • One tool for everything: match the tool to the task.
Match the review to the risk

Verification is part of the workflow

Low stakesQuick review
Medium stakesSource check + cross-check
High stakesAuthoritative source + expert review

AI is a capable assistant, not a substitute for professional judgement.

29 · Glossary

AI terms, in plain English

TermPlain-English meaning
LLMLarge language model—the text-trained engine behind many chat AIs.
PromptThe instruction or question you give the AI.
TokenA small chunk of text the model reads or writes—roughly a word or part of one.
Context windowHow much text the model can look at in one go, including your prompt, files, and reply.
HallucinationWhen AI invents something that sounds true but is not.
GroundingTying an answer to real sources you supplied or that were retrieved.
MultimodalCan handle combinations of text, images, audio, or video.

30 · Your 30-day action plan

Build one good habit at a time

Small experiments beat trying to master every tool at once.

Week 1 · Learn one assistant well

Practise prompts, files, search, and verification.

Week 2 · Add research and document tools

Practise checking sources and grounding answers.

Week 3 · Build one workflow

Try a prompt chain, a reusable template, or a simple automation.

Week 4 · Evaluate and share

Use a simple rubric, note what works, and share your best prompt.

Make it personal

My commitments

The one-page philosophy: Decide what to delegate, which tool to use, how to describe the task, what context to provide, how to structure the workflow, how to evaluate the output, when to verify, when not to use AI, and how to automate responsibly.
